## Why W&B matters

Weight and balance is a mandatory preflight calculation — not optional paperwork. It has two independent failure modes that can each be fatal:

- **Exceeding maximum gross weight** degrades every aspect of performance: longer takeoff roll, reduced climb rate, higher stall speed, longer landing distance, and possible structural overload. The aircraft was not certified for that weight.
- **CG outside limits** affects longitudinal stability and control. A forward CG requires up-elevator force to maintain level flight and may leave insufficient elevator authority to flare on landing. An aft CG reduces longitudinal stability and in the extreme can make a stall or spin unrecoverable.

Both must be checked before every flight, for the planned load at takeoff and after fuel burn.

## Key terms

**Datum** — An arbitrary reference plane defined in the aircraft's Type Certificate Data Sheet (TCDS). All arm measurements are made from this point.

**Arm** — The horizontal distance in inches from the datum to the point where a weight acts. Weights forward of the datum have a negative arm; weights aft have a positive arm.

**Moment** — Weight multiplied by arm (in lb × in). Moments are additive. The sum of all moments divided by the total weight gives the center of gravity.

**Center of Gravity (CG)** — The point where the aircraft would balance if suspended. Calculated as:

> CG = Total Moment ÷ Total Weight

**Useful load** — Maximum gross weight minus empty weight. This is the total available for pilots, passengers, baggage, fuel, and oil.

**Loading envelope** — A chart in the POH showing the range of CG positions (in inches from the datum) that are legal at each gross weight. The aircraft must remain inside this envelope throughout the flight.

## The C172S loading envelope

The chart below shows the normal category and utility category envelopes for a Cessna 172S (PLACEHOLDER values — see disclaimer). The x-axis is CG position in inches from the datum. The y-axis is gross weight in pounds.

The normal envelope is the larger region (blue outline). The utility envelope (purple outline) is a smaller subset that must be used for intentional maneuvers like spins and steep turns in the utility category. To remain in the utility category the aircraft must be lighter and the CG must stay closer to the forward portion of the envelope.

The colored dot on the chart shows your current computed CG and weight. Green means legal; red means out of limits.

## Try it: load the aircraft

Move the sliders to see how loading choices affect the CG and whether the aircraft stays within its envelope. Notice that rear passengers and aft baggage move the CG aft, while heavier front-seat loading moves it forward. Fuel at arm 48 in is aft of the typical loaded CG, so as fuel burns the CG tends to shift forward.
