## How this scenario works

This simulator walks you through a complete VFR departure sequence — seven steps, from getting the ATIS through contacting Departure Control after takeoff. Each step puts you in a real communications situation:

- **Listen** to the ATC side (press **Play** on any step to hear it spoken aloud, or replay it at any time)
- **Type** your pilot transmission when it is your turn to talk
- **See your grade** — the engine checks whether you included each required readback item and tells you exactly what you got right and what you missed
- Use **Show example** at any time before submitting if you need a hint

There are no tricks. Work through it at your own pace. If you fail a step, you can retry it or skip ahead and come back to it.

## Before you start: what to expect

The scenario covers the full ground-to-departure flow for a VFR departure out of a Class D airport:

1. **Get the ATIS** — Listen to Runway City information Alpha. No readback required.
2. **Call Ground** — Initiate contact with Ground Control from the FBO ramp. Includes all four parts of a standard initial call.
3. **Receive taxi clearance** — Ground gives you a runway and routing. Read back the runway and hold-short instruction.
4. **Call Tower at the hold short** — Contact Tower when you are ready to depart. Pilot-initiated call.
5. **Receive takeoff clearance** — Tower clears you for takeoff with departure instructions. Read back the runway, heading, and departure frequency.
6. **Contact Departure** — After becoming airborne, check in with Departure Control on the assigned frequency.
7. **Departure radar contact** — Departure gives you a final transmission. No readback; scenario ends.

## Debrief: what the FAA actually requires you to read back

The grader deducts points for each required readback item you omit. Here is the official list of what must come back in your transmission — not just what is polite, but what is **required by regulation or FAA policy**:

**Taxi clearances (AIM 4-3-18)**
- **Runway assignment** — always read back the runway you are cleared to taxi to
- **Hold-short instructions** — always read back any instruction to hold short of a runway, including the runway designation

**On first contact with any facility (AIM 7-2-3)**
- **Altimeter setting** — when ATC passes the altimeter, your first readback should include it; it confirms you have the current local setting

**Takeoff clearance (AIM 4-3-14)**
- **Runway** — confirm you are departing the correct runway
- **Assigned heading** — if Tower assigns a departure heading, read it back
- **Departure frequency** — if Tower hands you off ("contact Departure on 124.0"), include the frequency in your readback

The rationale behind these requirements is straightforward: a closed-loop readback lets the controller catch any error before it becomes a runway incursion or a traffic conflict. The runway and hold-short items are especially safety-critical because an incorrect readback — or no readback at all — can put you on an active runway without clearance.
