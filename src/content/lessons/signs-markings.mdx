## Why signs matter

Runway incursions — unauthorized aircraft or vehicles on an active runway — are one of the leading contributors to fatal accidents in general aviation. The FAA classifies runway incursions by severity: Category A is a near-miss or collision; Category D is the most benign. Even a Category D event can spiral into a catastrophe in seconds.

The airport sign system is your primary defense. Every color, shape, and position carries a legally-binding meaning. Getting it wrong is not an error in procedure — it may put your aircraft on an active runway in front of a landing jet.

## The 6 sign types — quick reference

| Type | Background | Text color | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mandatory** | Red | White | You must take a specific action (hold short, no entry) |
| **Location** | Black | Yellow | Where you ARE — current taxiway or runway |
| **Direction** | Yellow | Black | Where to GO — intersection routing with arrows |
| **Destination** | Yellow | Black | Route to a named area (FBO, terminal, runup) |
| **Information** | Yellow | Black | Operational guidance (noise abatement, frequencies) |
| **Distance Remaining** | Black | White | Runway distance left, in thousands of feet |

The two easiest confusions are location vs. direction. The memory trick: **black tells you where you ARE, yellow tells you where to GO.** Location signs (black background) are mounted alongside a direction sign at intersections so you always know both simultaneously.

## Mandatory signs in depth

Mandatory signs are the most critical category. You will encounter them in four situations:

1. **Runway hold-short bars** — the sign matches the runway designation you are approaching (e.g., "18-36"). The associated pavement markings are four yellow lines (two solid, two dashed). The solid lines face you — you stop before them.
2. **ILS critical area boundaries** — marked "ILS" or "CRITICAL AREA." Do not enter when the ILS is in use unless cleared.
3. **No entry signs** — a solid red sign with white horizontal bar: do not enter.
4. **Runway approach area signs** — indicate areas where entry requires ATC clearance even if no hold-short bars are painted.

The absolute rule: **never cross a hold-short line without an explicit ATC clearance, and always read back every runway hold-short instruction.** No exception. If ATC says "taxi to runway 18 via Alpha," that instruction does not include crossing runway 9/27 — it means hold short of 9/27 and wait for a separate crossing clearance.

## Location vs. Direction signs

At most intersections you will see a combined sign post with both types:

- The **location sign** (black background, yellow text) is on the right side of the post. It shows where you currently are.
- The **direction sign** (yellow background, black text, with arrows) is on the left side. It shows available routes.

Arrows on direction signs point in the direction you must turn to reach that taxiway or runway. An arrow pointing left means turn left; straight arrow means continue ahead.

**Destination signs** use the same color scheme as direction signs but point to named locations rather than taxiway designators. Common examples: FBO, RAMP, CARGO, TERMINAL. They include a straight arrow or angled arrow.

## Distance remaining markers

Distance remaining signs sit alongside the runway edge, counting down from the far end. They show remaining landing or takeoff distance in **thousands of feet**. A "3" sign means 3,000 ft of pavement remains ahead. The last marker is "1" — only 1,000 ft left to stop or accelerate.

These signs are especially important during rejected takeoffs and long landing rollouts. Knowing your aircraft's accelerate-stop distance and comparing it to the remaining markers is a real-time safety check.

## Hotspot awareness at TEACH1

Runway City Airport (TEACH1) has one designated hotspot:

**HS1 — Taxiway Alpha crossing of Runway 09/27.** This area (nodes `twa_09_27_hs` through `twa_09_27_exit`) has a high risk of inadvertent runway incursion. When taxiing southbound on Alpha, you must stop at the hold-short bars for runway 9/27, obtain a crossing clearance, and verify the runway is clear before proceeding.

Hotspot locations for real airports are published in the FAA Chart Supplement (formerly Airport/Facility Directory) and depicted on airport diagrams with a circled "HS" number. Brief yourself on hotspots before taxiing at any unfamiliar airport.
